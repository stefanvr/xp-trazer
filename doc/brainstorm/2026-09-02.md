# Brainstorm — 2026-09-02

**Draft. Binds nothing.** A record of an interview, not a decision and not a design. Nothing here may
be cited as authority; the specifications that consume it are the truth once they exist.

**Session complete.** Five topics: the core play, what progress remembers, what failing does, what is
in a level, and how it looks. Coverage against the four specifications is named at the end rather than
filled in.

**Three kinds of entry, kept apart deliberately** — *said* by the owner, *suggested* by the agent and
accepted, *open*. By the next session those read identically unless they are separated here.

---

## Feeds the first-version scope

Step 2 of the bootstrap owns this. Seeded here because the owner was warm, and because both answers
narrow the game rather than describing it.

### Said

- **The edge of version one is that a game starts.** *"Being able to start a game"* is what done
  means, not a description of the hook. Everything below is after that.
- **Version one is one level, with no mode.** Neither arcade nor journey — modes are a frame around a
  game that does not exist yet.

## Feeds spec-domain

### Said

- **A hazard is a placeable element, exactly like a brick.** It can be anywhere in the level rather
  than fixed to an edge or a boundary. Named as the thing that stuck from Traz on the C64.
- **A hazard costs the ball.** Touching one loses the ball; the hazard itself stays. It is a danger
  and permanent level furniture, not an obstacle the ball dismantles — so placeable hazards are a
  spatial threat played around for the whole level. *(Disambiguated by direct question, not
  volunteered.)*
- **Some bricks are permanent.** They are level structure, shaping the space the ball moves through,
  and clearing the level ignores them. *(Implied by "every brick that can be removed", surfaced by
  the agent, confirmed by the owner.)*
- **A level is cleared when every brick that can be removed has been removed.**
- **Bats are part of the level design**, not a fixed fitting the player brings to every level.
- **All horizontal bats move together, and all vertical bats move together.** Control is grouped by
  orientation, so a level with bats on two axes is driven as two things rather than as many.
- **Levels form a map, and the player chooses which direction to go.**
- **Clearing a level opens the surrounding levels.** Spatial, not a linear next.
- **A level can only be played once within a game run.**
- **Two modes, and the difference between them is how many runs they are.** *Arcade* is a single run:
  three lives, and when you die it ends. *Journey* is multiple runs, keeping track of which levels are
  unlocked.
- **Dying in journey mode returns you to selection.** You can then select the level you just lost, or
  any other unlocked one. Death costs the attempt, not the progress.
- **You can trace back over cleared levels.** A player cannot be stranded by having spent the levels
  around them.

### Suggested by the agent, accepted by the owner

- **The map is both movement and selection, in that order.** While progressing, the player occupies a
  position and picks a direction into an adjacent level; after dying, they choose freely from
  everything unlocked. This was the agent's reconciliation of two of the owner's own phrasings —
  *"choose in which direction"* and *"select this or any other unlocked level"* — put back as a choice
  and accepted, rather than something the owner stated unprompted. **Weaker evidence than the
  entries above, and it should stay looking weaker until a specification tests it.**

### Open

- **How the two bat groups are driven** — one input each, one input that switches, or something the
  level decides. The domain rule is settled; the control is not.
- **Whether a hazard can be removed by anything else.** Settled that the ball does not remove it;
  whether anything ever does is unasked.
- **How many balls are in play, and what losing one costs** outside arcade's three lives. A hazard
  costs the ball, so this now has consequences it did not have before.
- **Whether once-per-run resets between journey runs.** Unlocks persist across runs, so a cleared
  level is presumably playable again in a later run — stated for neither.
- **Whether the map is authored or generated**, and how big. Not asked, and no longer urgent: version
  one has no map.

## Feeds spec-app

### Said

- **Journey progress is persisted on the device, and not beyond it.** No carrying it between devices.
- **Level selection is a map**, chosen by direction — Traz's zone selection is where this comes from.
- **Selection is where the player lands after dying** in journey mode, so it is a place they return
  to constantly rather than a front-end they pass through once.

### Open

- **Input.** Never asked, and version one cannot exist without it — two bat groups on two axes is an
  input question before it is anything else.
- **Screens and flow.** Never asked.

## Feeds spec-style

### Said

- **Tron is the reference.** Named immediately, and without hesitation, as the screenshot it would be
  happy to be mistaken for.
- **The C64 is a flavour, not a constraint.** *"Just feels like it"* — no sixteen-colour rule, no
  palette discipline, no pixel grid to honour.

### Open

- **Whether sound is part of the feel or the last thing added.** The question was an either/or and the
  answer was *"yes"*, so it is not resolved. Sound matters; when it arrives does not follow.
- **What would make it visually wrong: *"no idea"*.** Asked and answered with don't-know, which is
  different from unasked. The visual direction is currently free, and free is worth knowing about —
  it is the cheapest kind of decision to move later.

## Feeds spec-tech

### Said, as a constraint rather than a choice

- **Device-local persistence is enough.** Journey progress needs no account and no server, which rules
  out a class of technology as unnecessary rather than ruling any in.
- **Version one needs no persistence at all**, since it has no mode and no map. The constraint above
  applies to the game, not to the first thing built.

## Tensions

### Left standing

- **Tron against the C64 heritage.** The visual anchor is Tron — neon on black, glow, line. The game's
  heritage is chunky C64 Traz and Arkanoid, and the README names that heritage rather than the look.
  *"Just feels like it"* stops them colliding outright, but they pull in different directions, and the
  first level has to look like one of them. Not put to the owner.

### Resolved since they were raised

- **Once-per-run against journey persistence.** Answered: a run is the unit, arcade is one of them and
  journey is many, with unlocks tracked across them. The residual question is only whether the
  once-only rule resets between runs.
- **Stranding.** Answered: cleared levels can be traced back over, so spending the levels around you
  is not a trap.
- **The game described against the first version described.** Answered by shrinking the first version
  rather than by trimming the game: version one is one level with no mode, and its edge is that a game
  starts. Everything else in this file is explicitly later. Kept here rather than deleted, because
  what was resolved is *which* of the two moved.

## Coverage, named rather than filled

- **spec-domain — strongest.** Bricks, permanent bricks, hazards, bats and their grouping, clearing,
  the map, the two modes and what death costs. Enough to write a first pass against.
- **spec-app — partial, and thin where version one needs it.** Persistence and selection are covered.
  Input and screens were never asked, and two bat groups on two axes makes input the first thing
  version one runs into.
- **spec-style — an anchor and a licence, nothing else.** Tron, and the C64 as flavour. No palette, no
  resolution, no sound placement, and no stated way to get it wrong.
- **spec-tech — constraints only, deliberately.** Nothing is chosen, which is correct: the choice is a
  goal of its own, proven by the smallest thing that exercises it.

## Asked three times, answered *"no idea"*

**What the game drops** relative to Traz and Arkanoid. Everything in this file adds, and by the
owner's own account no cut has been found yet. Recorded rather than dropped, because the scope step
inherits it: *don't build further than the goal in front of you needs* is the only thing currently
doing that job.
