# Brainstorm — 2026-09-03

**Draft. Binds nothing.** A record of an interview, not a decision and not a design. Nothing here may
be cited as authority; the routine that consumes it is the truth once it exists.

**Subject: how the owner and the agent work together on a specification** — not what the game is.
That is a deviation from the `brainstorm` skill, which groups its output by the specification that
will consume it. Nothing here feeds a specification; it feeds a routine. Grouped by activity instead.

**Three kinds of entry, kept apart deliberately** — *said* by the owner, *suggested* by the agent and
accepted, *open*. By the next session they read identically unless they are separated here.

---

## How spec-style actually got written

### Said

- **The agent offered a choice — write a proposal, or ask for input — and the owner took the proposal
  route.** *"Another you asked to do a proposal or give input, in this case I took the proposal route
  and accepted it."*
- **The acceptance was "that it was right"**, not indifference. Asked directly whether it had been
  *yes, that's right* or *yes, fine, I don't care*, the owner answered the first. This matters
  because the brainstorm had recorded the visual direction as *free* and *"no idea"*, which would
  have made a shrug the likelier answer.

## The activities inside a domain specification

### Said

- **Four, named unprompted:** *"Ubiquitous Language · high level, event storming · Business Rules
  (game rules in this case) · data modelling / reference data defining."*
- **"Maybe not all are always needed."**
- **Domain and app want different techniques, so they are independent.** *"Domain is more on the
  event storming side, app more story mapping thing, so can be independent."* This removes the seam
  the agent expected between them — flow had looked like it belonged to both at once.
- **A branch with multiple commits**, confirmed for the shape of the work.

### Suggested by the agent, accepted

- **That the right mode — who proposes — differs per activity**, rather than the activities merely
  being separate steps. Put as an either/or and answered *"yes that what I mean"*. **The principle is
  the owner's; the specific assignment below is the agent's and was not disputed rather than
  endorsed, so it should stay looking weaker until a routine tests it:** vocabulary is the owner's
  (the words are already theirs), event storming is the agent's to propose, the game rules are
  largely settled already in the 2026-09-02 brainstorm, and the data model is the agent's to propose
  because nothing in that brainstorm touches it.

## What goes wrong between a brainstorm and a specification

### Said

- **A brainstorm is raw thought, and may be inconsistent or incomplete.** *"The brainstorm might not
  be consistent / complete as it is also more raw thought. In this case it's much clear, but again
  with xp-garden that was not the case."*
- **Skipping the activities turns correction into whack-a-mole.** *"As the activities mention did not
  take place, scrutinizing names options rules, it became a wack mole on correcting."* Two examples,
  both from xp-garden: **a prune activity taking a mandatory plant, with the logic for picking it
  unexamined**; and **a weed action aimed at a specific weed taking free text**, where a weed
  arguably wanted an identity.
- **Revision inside the exercise is not the failure.** *"The whack a mole is a big problem if
  correction [takes] place on implementation and needs a constant spec update. Within the exercise of
  creating or extending the spec it can be a natural thing of better understanding, of uncovering in
  the process."* — This corrected the agent's framing, which had treated scrutiny as *get it right
  before writing anything*.
- **A second category of gap: flow.** *"More on the flow of things. For Trazer it is quite clean:
  pick a mode → play level → navigation logic based on mode → etc etc."* Clean here; *"with other
  brainstorms like the xp-garden app there was a bigger gap."*
- **A third category, and it belonged to neither specification.** *"Somewhere things like making it
  adjust to screen size, having things like topbar footer, [whether] something [is] in a menu was not
  touched upon both in domain or app spec. (yes minimal thing but still)"*
- **That third category is out of scope here, and is expected to recur.** *"3 was a xp-garden issue,
  the scope of Trazer excludes it, so it has not yet become a problem. For V2, without this
  discussion, I would expect the same issue as xp-garden."*

### Suggested by the agent, partially accepted

- **One scrutiny pass: for each rule, name the data it needs, and say whether that data is a modelled
  thing with an identity or free text.** Both xp-garden examples would surface. The owner's response
  placed it: *"I think this catches 1 category"* — so it is one pass among more than one, not the
  shape of the whole step.

## How a specification should read

### Said

- **The domain spec states the desired state.** *"Have a look on xp-garden, it got quite messy as it
  annotated unconfirm names. The domain spec should read as the desired state."*
- What that names, concretely, is `~/xp-garden/doc/spec-domain.md`'s vocabulary table carrying a
  **"Whose word"** column — rows reading *the owner's*, *coined*, *coined — the owner has not named
  this* — plus ⏳ markers for decided-but-unbuilt, alongside **Open items** and **Retired
  identifiers** sections. The table names the vocabulary and tracks its own confidence at the same
  time.

### A concern the agent raised and withdrew

The agent argued that `doc/spec-style.md` was at fault for not marking which parts the owner chose and
which an agent proposed — seven hex values among them. **The rule above answers it: spec-style is
right as it stands.** Provenance belongs to the brainstorm and to git history, not to a specification.
Recorded so the objection is not raised again from scratch.

## Open

- **Which of the four activities `spec-domain` needs for version one.** *"Maybe not all are always
  needed"* — asked, and deferred to this file rather than answered.
- **What story mapping looks like here.** Named for spec-app, and nothing beyond the name. Not asked.
- **The catch-mechanism for deferred surface concerns.** Established that they correctly wait, and
  that without something they will arrive unnoticed at v2 exactly as they did in xp-garden. What that
  something is was not discussed.
- **How many scrutiny passes there are, and what they are.** One is named above and explicitly is not
  the whole set; flow is a second category but no pass was described for it.

## Tensions

### Left standing

- **"Don't specify further than the goal in front of you needs" against things nobody owns until they
  are needed.** The rule correctly defers screen size, chrome and menus, and the owner agrees it was
  right to here — while expecting that same deferral to produce xp-garden's problem at v2. Both halves
  are held, and nothing yet reconciles them.

### Resolved, and worth recording as resolved

- **Provenance against a clean specification.** They do not compete once the boundary is named: the
  brainstorm separates *said* from *suggested* from *open*; the specification asserts. The cost is
  accepted rather than overlooked — a reader of a specification cannot tell what was invented, and
  the history is where that question is answered.

- **The scrutiny step**, beyond a single named pass and a second named category.
